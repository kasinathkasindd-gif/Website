## 1. Thiruvananthapuram (Kerala)

**Unique fact** — ✅ Verified  
Festival, not everyday: at Attukal Pongala, women cook pongala (a sweet rice offering) in earthen pots along the city's streets. Guinness recorded 2.5 million women on 10 Mar 2009, the largest annual gathering of women. Boli (thin sweet flatbread) eaten with payasam is a Thiruvananthapuram feast specialty. [S19; S20; S17; S18]

### Breakfast: Puttu (പുട്ട്) + Kadala curry (കടലക്കറി)
- **English:** Steamed rice-flour-and-coconut cylinders with black-chickpea curry
- **Ingredients:** Puttu: coarse roasted rice flour, grated coconut, salt, water. Kadala: black chickpeas, onion/shallots, tomato, ginger-garlic, green chilli, coconut (roasted & ground, or coconut milk), coriander-chilli-garam masala, curry leaves, coconut oil
- **Method:** Rice flour moistened with salted water to damp crumbs; packed into a puttu kutti (steaming tube) in layers with grated coconut; steamed over a boiling pot until steam escapes the top (~5-8 min). Chickpeas soaked overnight, pressure-cooked, then simmered with sauteed onion-tomato-spice and coconut paste (~25-30 min active).
- **Status:** 🟡 Partially verified · **Data basis:** Regional pattern: South Kerala (Travancore) · **Sources:** S01; S03
- **Notes:** Dosa/idli with sambar & chutney is also common in South Kerala homes (Tamil influence) - not yet confirmed by 2 sources.
- **Spice (editorial):** 2 (curry) steam puffs (0-3)

### Lunch: Choru (ചോറ്) + Meen curry (മീൻ കറി) + Thoran (തോരൻ) + Sambar or Moru curry + Pappadam + Achar
- **English:** Red parboiled rice with fish curry, coconut vegetable stir-fry, lentil stew or buttermilk curry, papad and pickle
- **Ingredients:** Kerala matta (red parboiled) rice; fish (often sardine or mackerel) with kudampuli or tamarind, chilli, shallots, ginger, garlic, fenugreek, curry leaves, coconut oil (ground coconut in some styles); thoran: chopped cabbage/beans/beetroot + grated coconut, turmeric, green chilli, mustard seed; moru: yogurt + coconut-cumin paste; pappadam; mango or lime pickle
- **Method:** Rice boiled in plenty of water (~30-40 min, or pressure-cooked) and drained; fish simmered with soaked kudampuli and spices, often in a manchatti clay pot on low-medium heat (~20 min); thoran stir-fried ~10 min; moru heated gently without boiling.
- **Status:** 🟡 Partially verified · **Data basis:** State pattern (no district-specific data found) · **Sources:** S08; S09; S16
- **Notes:** Coastal households likely eat more fish; to confirm locally.
- **Spice (editorial):** 2 steam puffs (0-3)

### Dinner: Kanji (കഞ്ഞി) + Cherupayar (ചെറുപയർ) + Chammanthi / Pappadam / Achar
- **English:** Red-rice gruel (congee) with green gram, coconut chutney, papad and pickle
- **Ingredients:** Matta rice, water, salt; green gram (moong) with coconut, shallots, chilli; coconut chammanthi (coconut, chilli, shallot, tamarind); pappadam; pickle
- **Method:** Rice cooked very soft in plenty of water (~40 min in a pot, less in a pressure cooker) and served with its starchy water; green gram boiled then tossed with coconut and a mustard-curry-leaf tempering.
- **Status:** 🟡 Partially verified · **Data basis:** State pattern (no district-specific data found) · **Sources:** S05; S06; S07
- **Notes:** Chapati at dinner is a growing pattern statewide (rice consumption falling, wheat rising per HCES coverage) but not district-confirmed.
- **Spice (editorial):** 0-1 steam puffs (0-3)

## 4. Alappuzha (Kerala)

**Unique fact** — ✅ Verified  
In Kuttanad, families farm rice BELOW sea level (about 1-3 m; sources vary) on land drained from the Vembanad wetland and held back by bunds. FAO named it a Globally Important Agricultural Heritage System in 2013. After harvest, ducks are herded into the paddies. [S28; S29]

### Lunch: Choru (ചോറ്) + Kudampuli meen curry + Thoran + Pappadam
- **English:** Red parboiled rice with a Malabar-tamarind fish curry, coconut stir-fry and papad
- **Ingredients:** Matta rice; local fish (pearl spot is the prized one, sardine/mackerel everyday) with kudampuli, chilli, shallots, ginger, garlic, curry leaves, coconut oil; thoran vegetables + coconut; pappadam
- **Method:** Rice boiled and drained (~30-40 min); fish simmered with soaked kudampuli and spices in a clay pot (~20 min); thoran stir-fried (~10 min).
- **Status:** ✅ Verified · **Data basis:** Sub-regional: Kuttanad · **Sources:** S28; S29; S17; S16
- **Notes:** FAO notes Kuttanad rice is the farmers' staple and the system integrates rice, fish and ducks (S28, S29); Onmanorama's district tour describes fish and duck on daily Alappuzha menus (S17).

## 5. Kottayam (Kerala)

**Unique fact** — 🟡 Partially verified (1 press source + recipe authors)  
Kottayam-style fish curry (meen vevichathu / mulakittathu) is fiery red, soured with kudampuli (Malabar tamarind, Garcinia gummi-gutta), uses no coconut and is cooked in a clay manchatti. It is made to keep: cooks say it tastes better the next day. [S33; S16; S32]

### Breakfast: Appam / Palappam (അപ്പം) + Stew (സ്റ്റ്യൂ)
- **English:** Lacy fermented rice pancakes with mild coconut-milk stew (vegetable, egg or chicken)
- **Ingredients:** Appam: raw rice, coconut, a little cooked rice, sugar, salt, yeast (traditionally toddy). Stew: potato/carrot/beans or egg/chicken, onion, ginger, green chilli, whole spices (cinnamon, clove, cardamom, pepper), thin + thick coconut milk, curry leaves, coconut oil
- **Method:** Rice ground with coconut and cooked rice, fermented overnight; a ladle of batter swirled in an appachatti (small curved pan) so the edges go lacy, covered ~2 min, never flipped. Stew simmered in thin coconut milk with whole spices, finished with thick coconut milk off the boil (~25-30 min).
- **Status:** 🟡 Partially verified · **Data basis:** District-specific (1 source) + state pattern · **Sources:** S03; S04; S32; S34
- **Notes:** Onmanorama names soft lacy appams a speciality of Kottayam (S32). Strongly associated with Syrian Christian homes but common across central Kerala.
- **Spice (editorial):** 1 steam puffs (0-3)

### Lunch: Choru + Meen vevichathu / Meen mulakittathu (മീൻ മുളകിട്ടത്) + Thoran + Moru
- **English:** Red parboiled rice with fiery red, kudampuli-soured fish curry (no coconut), coconut stir-fry and buttermilk curry
- **Ingredients:** Matta rice; fish (sardine, mackerel, seer), kudampuli (soaked), Kashmiri + hot chilli powder, shallots, ginger, garlic, fenugreek, curry leaves, coconut oil
- **Method:** Spice paste fried in coconut oil in a seasoned manchatti; water and kudampuli added; fish simmered on low-medium heat (~15-20 min) - high heat cracks clay pots; rested; keeps for days and is said to improve.
- **Status:** 🟡 Partially verified · **Data basis:** District-specific (1 press source + recipe authors) · **Sources:** S33; S16; S32
- **Notes:** Kottayam-style curry: red, kudampuli-soured, no coconut, cooked in a manchatti (S33).
- **Spice (editorial):** 3 steam puffs (0-3)

### Dinner: Kappa (കപ്പ) + Meen curry (മീൻ കറി)
- **English:** Boiled or mashed tapioca (cassava) with fish curry
- **Ingredients:** Tapioca root, salt, turmeric; for puzhukku/vevichathu: grated coconut, green chilli, shallots, garlic, curry leaves, mustard seed, coconut oil; red fish curry
- **Method:** Tapioca peeled, cubed and boiled in salted water until soft (~20-25 min), drained; eaten plain or mashed with crushed coconut-chilli-shallot; served with fish curry.
- **Status:** 🟡 Partially verified · **Data basis:** State pattern (no district-specific data found) · **Sources:** S12; S13
- **Notes:** Leftover fish curry with kappa or rice is typical; kanji or chapati are alternatives.
- **Spice (editorial):** 2-3 steam puffs (0-3)

## 6. Idukki (Kerala)

**Unique fact** — 🟡 Partially verified (share figures conflict)  
Idukki grows most of India's small (green) cardamom. A 2024 Spices Board brochure gives 22,868.54 t, about 90% of national output; another report puts all of Kerala at ~60% of India, so the exact share is disputed. Local favourites: 'Asiad' (beef with tapioca) and sun-dried 'idi irachi' beef. [S35; S36; S37]

## 7. Ernakulam (Kerala)

**Unique fact** — ✅ Verified

It is farmed in rotation with prawns: rice in the monsoon, prawns in the dry months feeding on the stubble. Its area shrank from ~24,000 ha to under 6,000 ha. [S38; S39]
