## 1. Leh (Ladakh (UT))

**Unique fact** — ✅ Verified  
Butter tea (gur-gur cha) is salty, not sweet: tea churned with butter and salt. Rice was once a luxury in Ladakh; subsidised rice from the plains is now a staple, partly displacing local barley (S69; debated in S70). [S68; S69; S70; S72; S73]

### Breakfast: Gur-gur cha (butter tea) + Khambir
- **English:** Salted butter tea with thick local wheat bread
- **Ingredients:** Tea, water, salt, butter, milk; khambir: whole-wheat flour, sourdough/yeast, salt
- **Method:** Tea boiled long, then churned with butter and salt in a tall wooden churn (or blender) until frothy; khambir dough rested and baked or griddled into a thick round.
- **Status:** 🟡 Partially verified · **Data basis:** District-specific (Ladakh) · **Sources:** S68; S82
- **Notes:** Native-script name: [Bhoti/Purgi script - reviewer to supply]. Only one countable academic source names khambir; travel sources (pointer only) agree. Kholak (barley flour kneaded with butter tea) is an equally common breakfast.
- **Spice (editorial):** 0 steam puffs (0-3)

### Lunch: Daal-chawal + vegetables
- **English:** Rice and lentils with seasonal vegetables (subsidised rice is now common)
- **Ingredients:** Rice (often from the public distribution system), lentils, potato, turnip, greens
- **Method:** Rice boiled or pressure-cooked; dal simmered with tomato and spices; vegetables stir-cooked.
- **Status:** 🟡 Partially verified · **Data basis:** District-specific (rice shift) + traditional pattern · **Sources:** S69; S70
- **Notes:** Native-script name: [Bhoti/Purgi script - reviewer to supply]. Traditional alternative: paba with tangtur (S94, S95, S68). The rice-for-barley shift is documented (S69); causes debated (S70).
- **Spice (editorial):** 1 steam puffs (0-3)

### Dinner: Thukpa
- **English:** Noodle soup with vegetables and/or meat
- **Ingredients:** Wheat noodles (hand-made or bought), onion, garlic, ginger, turnip/greens, sometimes mutton or yak meat; salt
- **Method:** Broth simmered with vegetables (and meat); hand-pulled or rolled noodles cooked in it (~20-30 min).
- **Status:** 🟡 Partially verified · **Data basis:** Regional pattern: Ladakh · **Sources:** S71; S68
- **Notes:** Native-script name: [Bhoti/Purgi script - reviewer to supply]. Dinner is the least documented meal; confirm with a local reviewer.
- **Spice (editorial):** 1 steam puffs (0-3)

## 2. Changthang (Ladakh (UT))

**Unique fact** — ✅ Verified  
On the high Changthang plateau, nomadic Changpa families rely almost entirely on meat and dairy; a 2022 study reports a family may eat one yak or 3-4 sheep/goats per winter. The Changpas are among India's foremost producers of pashmina (cashmere) wool. [S82; S83]

### Breakfast: Tsampa + Butter tea
- **English:** Roasted-barley flour eaten with salted butter tea (or plain butter)
- **Ingredients:** Roasted barley flour, butter tea (tea, salt, butter, milk) or butter
- **Method:** Barley flour eaten mixed into butter tea or with butter; butter tea churned as above.
- **Status:** ✅ Verified · **Data basis:** District-specific (Changthang study) + Ladakh pattern · **Sources:** S82; S68
- **Notes:** Native-script name: [Bhoti/Purgi script - reviewer to supply]. Nomadic (Changpa), semi-nomadic and settled households eat differently (S82).
- **Spice (editorial):** 0 steam puffs (0-3)

### Lunch: Meat and dairy meal (e.g. mutton/yak stew with churpe)
- **English:** Meat stew with dried cheese and dairy - the nomads' mainstay
- **Ingredients:** Sheep, goat or yak meat; churpe/chura (dried cheese); butter; barley flour
- **Method:** Meat simmered into a stew or soup; dried cheese eaten as is or softened in tea. Exact household recipes (e.g. sha-spaqs, gyuma) to be confirmed with a local reviewer.
- **Status:** 🟡 Partially verified · **Data basis:** District-specific (1 academic source) · **Sources:** S82
- **Notes:** Native-script name: [Bhoti/Purgi script - reviewer to supply]. Nomads depend solely on meat and dairy; semi-nomads mix meat, dairy and other foods (S82). Specific dish names need a local reviewer.
- **Spice (editorial):** 1 steam puffs (0-3)

### Dinner: Thukpa
- **English:** Noodle soup with vegetables and/or meat
- **Ingredients:** Wheat noodles (hand-made or bought), onion, garlic, ginger, turnip/greens, sometimes mutton or yak meat; salt
- **Method:** Broth simmered with vegetables (and meat); hand-pulled or rolled noodles cooked in it (~20-30 min).
- **Status:** 🟡 Partially verified · **Data basis:** Regional pattern: Ladakh · **Sources:** S71; S68
- **Notes:** Native-script name: [Bhoti/Purgi script - reviewer to supply]. Meat thukpa is plausible for herding families; unconfirmed.
- **Spice (editorial):** 1 steam puffs (0-3)

## 8. Zanskar (Ladakh (UT))

**Unique fact** — 🟡 Partially verified (sources are Ladakh-wide)  
Like the rest of Ladakh, Zanskar is cut off by snow for months each year; families rely on stored grains and vegetables (traditional pits and stores), and thud - a brick of butter and dried cheese - is common in Changthang and Zanskar. [S68; S97]

### Breakfast: Kholak (ngamphe/tsampa kneaded with butter tea)
- **English:** Roasted-barley flour mixed into butter tea - a no-cook breakfast
- **Ingredients:** Roasted huskless barley flour (ngamphe/tsampa), butter tea (or butter)
- **Method:** Hot butter tea poured onto barley flour in a bowl and kneaded by hand into a dough; no cooking.
- **Status:** 🟡 Partially verified · **Data basis:** Regional pattern: Ladakh (Zanskar source is about monks) · **Sources:** S68; S82; S98
- **Notes:** Native-script name: [Bhoti/Purgi script - reviewer to supply]. A Zanskar monastery account describes kholak (S98) - not average households.
