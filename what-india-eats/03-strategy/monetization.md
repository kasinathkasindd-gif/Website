# Phase 4.5: Monetization plan

## 1. YouTube Partner Program (YPP): rules on 18 Nov 2026, and the change coming on 1 Feb 2027

| Tier | Until 31 Jan 2027 | From **1 Feb 2027** (new applicants) | What it unlocks |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Expanded YPP** | 500 subs + 3 public uploads in 90 days + (3,000 watch hours/12 mo **or** 3 M Shorts views/90 days) | Unchanged | Memberships, Super Thanks/Stickers, Shopping (fan funding). **No ad revenue.** |
| **Full YPP** | 1,000 subs + (4,000 watch hours/12 mo **or** 10 M Shorts views/90 days) | 1,000 subs + (**8,000** watch hours/365 days **or** **20 M** Shorts views/90 days) | Ad revenue sharing, Shorts Creator Pool |
| **Shorts Creator Pool payout** | YPP member | Must also keep **10 M qualified Shorts views per trailing 90 days** to earn from the pool that month | Shorts ad revenue |

Sources:
- P01 and P02 (current rules)
- P03 (Shorts revenue model)
- P04, an official YouTube blog (2027 changes). Third-party summaries agree (P05), but they differ on how existing partners are grandfathered, so re-check P04 in January 2027.

The base requirements still apply: AdSense, 2-step verification, an eligible country, no active strikes, and compliance with the monetization policies.

**What this means for us:** the channel will almost certainly apply under the **2027 rules**.
- 20 M Shorts views in 90 days is a high bar.
- **8,000 long-form watch hours** may be the easier route. That is why every state finale is a long-form compilation, and every Short links to it through the **Related video** feature.
  - Example: 1,250 hours/month from compilations reaches 8,000 in about 6–7 months.

## 2. What Shorts actually pay (set expectations honestly)

**How the pool works (official, P03):**
- Shorts Feed ad revenue is pooled every month.
- Music licensing takes its cut first. **Our original music keeps the whole share in the creator pool.**
- The pool is split by each creator's share of engaged views, and the creator keeps **45%**.

**What it pays in practice (third-party estimates, P20; varies a lot):**
- Shorts RPM ≈ **$0.02–0.07 per 1,000 views** for India-heavy audiences.
- About **$0.18–0.33** for US/UK-heavy audiences.

**Illustration (not a forecast):** 10 M views per month with a 50% India / 50% US-UK mix ≈ 5 M × $0.04 + 5 M × $0.25 ≈ **$1,450/month**.

So Shorts ad revenue is the *floor*, not the business. Long-form food content typically earns several dollars per 1,000 views from US audiences (P20), which is another reason for the compilations.

## 5. How to scale

1. **Throughput:** the batch pipeline (`06-production/low-credit-plan.md`) handles a whole state per week. Verification is the bottleneck, so add **paid local reviewers per state** (food writers or home cooks; ₹500–1,500 per district check is a starting assumption).
2. **Languages:** auto-dubbing first. If the analytics show demand, add dedicated Hindi and Spanish channels later, using the same assets and new VO only.
3. **Authenticity:**
   - Bring in real families (with consent) as photos, voice notes or recipe credits through "Invite Seetu".
   - This raises trust, satisfies YouTube's "original content" expectation, and strengthens verification.
4. **Formats beyond Shorts:**
   - State finales (long-form).
   - A "Neighbours" comparison series (two adjacent districts side by side).
   - "How India's plate is changing" mini-docs (rice vs wheat, PDS rice in Ladakh).
5. **Policy safety:**
   - Keep the animation clearly non-realistic. Animation does not need an AI label (P06).
   - If photorealistic AI footage is ever used, tick *altered or synthetic content*; since May 2026 YouTube applies labels automatically when it detects undisclosed realistic AI (P07).
