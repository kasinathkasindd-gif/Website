# Phase 2: Presenting Indian home food to a global audience

## 1. What the evidence says (and what it doesn't)

| Finding | Evidence | What it means for us |
|---|---|---|
| Indian food is already liked worldwide, especially in the UK (84%), Saudi Arabia (76%), Norway (75%), Australia (74%), Singapore (77%) and the UAE (71%), and least in China (26%) and Thailand (27%) | YouGov survey: 25,000+ people, 24 countries, 34 cuisines; Indian ranked 9th (P16, 2019; this is old data) | First targets for growth outside India: UK, Gulf, Australia, Singapore/Malaysia, Nordics. Treat China and Thailand as low priority. |
| There is a huge built-in diaspora: **37.28 million overseas Indians**; the US hosts the largest population of Indian origin and the UAE the largest NRI population | MEA, January 2026 (P17) | Diaspora viewers are the seed audience in the US and the Gulf. They also share widely ("this is my grandmother's district!"). |
| Indian village and home cooking can reach a global scale: Village Cooking Channel has about **31 M subscribers** | vidIQ, 4 Nov 2026 (P19) | Home and rural authenticity works. There is no country breakdown, so we can't claim a "% foreign" figure. |
| Most Indians are **not** vegetarian: 83.4% of men and 70.6% of women (aged 15–49) eat non-veg at least occasionally | NFHS-5 analyses (P18) | A strong myth-buster hook for foreign viewers, who often assume "India = vegetarian". State it carefully and with its source. |
| A home-cooked vegetarian thali costs **₹29.1** (July 2026) and a non-veg one **₹58.2** (June 2026) | CRISIL Roti Rice Rate (P14, P15) | "Price of a meal" hook. Show ₹ first, then about US$, with the date. Never invent a district price. |
| Kerala's meal culture is shifting: rice consumption is down (rural 7.39 → 5.82 kg/month, 2011-12 → 2022-23) as wheat rises | HCES coverage (S10) | "How India's plate is changing" episodes: chapati at dinner, PDS rice replacing barley in Ladakh (S69). |

**Gaps, stated plainly:**
- I found **no public dataset** that shows the share of foreign viewers on Indian food channels, or Shorts retention by country. The strategy below is therefore built from the evidence above plus well-established Shorts mechanics.
- After 30 days, YouTube Studio's *Audience → Top geographies* and *retention* reports replace these assumptions with your own data.

## 2. The angles that make non-Indians curious (ranked for this series)

1. **Surprise: "India eats *this*?"** Facts that break stereotypes, used as the 0–2 s hook:
   - Salty butter tea (Leh)
   - Rice farmed below sea level (Alappuzha)
   - Smoked pork and a chilli 100× hotter than a jalapeño (Kohima)
   - Fermented "water" crêpes (Kasaragod)
2. **A day of eating.** Three meals in about 45 s is the proven "what X eats in a day" format, applied to whole districts. It needs zero explanation for global viewers.
3. **Comparison with their own food, using respectful bridges and never "Indian version of X":**
   - *kanji ≈ congee* (Onmanorama itself makes this bridge, S05)
   - *skyu ≈ hand-pressed pasta stew*
   - *appam ≈ a lacy, fermented crêpe*
   - Every bridge ends with a question to the viewer: "What's YOUR breakfast?"
4. **Regional contrast.** Because of the route, neighbouring districts are back to back. Pair them ("Alappuzha farms below sea level; next door, Kottayam's fish curry tastes better on day two"). Global viewers learn "India is not one cuisine".
5. **Price of a meal.** Only with dated, sourced numbers (CRISIL thali; dated local-canteen prices such as S67). Keep it to a respectful wonder ("a full home-cooked plate for about 30 US cents"), never poverty framing.
6. **Process and sound.** Coconut scraping, mustard seeds popping in hot oil, the cooker whistle. Sound-led cooking moments are universally watchable.

**Angles to avoid:**
- "Street-food hygiene shock" and "spicy challenge" pranks. They pull hostile comments and conflict with our home-kitchen premise.
- "Cheapest food" poverty framing.
- Anything that ranks one region over another.

## 3. Language, subtitles, narration and on-screen text

| Element | Recommendation | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Narration language** | English, light natural Indian accent | The widest reach. The accent signals authenticity; a caricature accent would not. |
| **Auto-dubbing** | Turn on YouTube auto-dubbing when eligible (YPP channels; P09, P10). Prioritise Spanish, Portuguese, Indonesian, Arabic, Hindi, French and German. | Up to 27 languages without extra production. Review the dubbed dish names, because they can be mispronounced. |
| **Titles & descriptions** | Add translated titles and descriptions in YouTube Studio for those same languages | It's free and lifts search in those markets. |
| **Burned-in captions** | Always. 2 lines max, about 5–7 words per line, high contrast, inside the safe zone. Sync captions to the voice word by word. | Many viewers in the Gulf, SE Asia and Europe are second-language English speakers, and some watch muted. |
| **Dish name cards** | Three layers: native script → romanised → English meaning. Example: **പുട്ട് · puttu · steamed rice-and-coconut cylinder** | Respect for locals, learning for foreigners, and searchable names. |
| **Numbers** | Metric first, then imperial for US viewers (3,500 m / 11,500 ft); ₹ first, then about US$ with the conversion date in the description | Accuracy and global readability. |
| **Narration style** | A curious friend, not a lecturer: short sentences, questions, one fact per beat, about 2.7 words/s. Every factual sentence is traceable to the data table. | Retention, and the accuracy rule. |
| **Sources** | Description lists the 2–4 key sources for that episode's facts and links the open data table | Credibility, and a defence against "this is wrong" comment pile-ons. |

## 4. On-screen text system (the same every episode, so viewers learn it)

| Type | Position | Max length | Style |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hook text | Top third, 0–2 s | 6 words | Huge, white on a dark box, one key word in turmeric yellow |
| Meal header | Top-left chip | 1 word ("BREAKFAST") + emoji clock | Chilli-red chip |
| Dish card | Lower third, above the Shorts UI | 3 lines (script / roman / English) | White card, ink text |
| Steam-o-meter (spice) | Top-right | 0–3 steam-puff icons | Animated puff per level; Rocket Mode overflows the meter |
| Fact card | Centre, with the lid-pop | 12 words + tiny source tag (e.g. "FAO, 2013") | Yellow card |
| Counter | End card | "District 4 / 793" | Map board |

**Safe zone:** keep text out of the bottom ~20% (caption, title, music bar) and the right ~12% (like/comment buttons) of the 1080×1920 frame.
