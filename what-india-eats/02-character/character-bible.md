# Character bible: SEETU, the pressure cooker who whistles for every meal in India

> **Logline:** A 32-year-old pressure cooker leaves the family kitchen shelf to eat breakfast, lunch and dinner with a family in every district of India, and whistles once for every meal.

I designed Seetu from scratch for this series. It is not based on any existing character, mascot, brand or real person (see the originality check at the end). It is a stylised, generic kitchen object: no brand logos, no brand colours, no brand jingles.

---

## 1. Why a pressure cooker stops the scroll

| What stops a thumb in 1–2 s | How Seetu delivers it |
|---|---|
| A sound pattern interrupt | Frame 0 is a sharp **cooker whistle** with a steam burst. Indian viewers recognise it instantly, and for others it's novel and funny. |
| A face looking at the camera | Big glossy eyes on a shiny round body, popping into frame and staring straight at the viewer. |
| An unusual silhouette | A round pot, a long sideways handle "ponytail", a red whistle "topknot" and a flapping yellow scarf. It reads even as a black shadow. |
| A question in the viewer's head | "Why is a pressure cooker talking… and what's it about to eat?" |
| Built into the format | The **whistle count is the episode structure**: 1 whistle after breakfast, 2 after lunch, 3 after dinner. Indian kitchens time dal and rice "for three whistles", so the signature sign-off writes itself. |

## 2. Identity

| Field | Choice |
|---|---|
| **Name** | **Seetu** (SEE-too). From *seeti*, Hindi/Urdu for "whistle". Two syllables, easy to say in any language, and a friendly nickname shape like Chintu or Bittu. |
| Backup names (if you dislike Seetu) | *Kooker* (Indian-English for cooker) · *Teeno* (from *teen*, "three" in Hindi, for the three whistles) |
| Age | **32 "cooker-years"**: made in 1994, which is ancient for a cooker. Seetu jokes about it ("Back in my day, we had ONE gasket and we were grateful."). |
| Gender | Deliberately unspecified; scripts use "Seetu" or "they" if needed. This keeps the character welcome in every home and avoids gender stereotypes about kitchens. |
| Species | A living stovetop pressure cooker, about 3-litre proportions, roughly knee-high to an adult. |
| Home | "A small kitchen shelf somewhere in India." This is kept vague on purpose so Seetu belongs to no single region, religion or caste. |

## 3. Personality

- **Curious first, hungry second.** Asks the questions a foreign viewer is thinking: "Wait, you eat rice for *breakfast*?"
- **A polite guest.** Always thanks the cook, never calls food weird, takes shoes off. Indian hospitality is shown, not explained.
- **Dramatic about spice.** Steams, rattles and occasionally launches.
- **A stickler for facts.** Double-checks everything (the running bit "Every day… or just for guests?"). This puts the show's accuracy ethic into the character.
- **Old-fashioned but game.** Nervous on boats and shared jeeps, proud of trains.
- **Never mean, never mocking.** The humour is always at Seetu's expense, never at the family's or the food's.

## 4. Catchphrases

| Use | Line | Why it works |
|---|---|---|
| **Main catchphrase (every episode's end)** | **"Three whistles — done!"** | A pan-Indian kitchen in-joke ("put the dal on for three whistles") that intrigues foreign viewers. It is chantable and pairs with the sonic logo. |
| Opener pattern | "What's cooking, *[District]*?" | A global idiom plus the district name in the first 2 seconds, which helps search. |
| Honesty check (running gag) | "Every day… or just for guests?" | Separates everyday food from festival food on screen, which is the research rule as a joke. |
| Extra-helping surrender | "No no no… okay. Just one more." | The universal grandma joke, and a natural lead-in to "just one more district". |

## 5. Look

**Body shape:** a squat, round-bellied aluminium pressure cooker that is wider at the belly than the lid.
- **The lid is the "head-top".** It is a gently domed outer lid. The **red whistle** (pressure weight) sits on top like a topknot.
- **The long black handle** sticks out to Seetu's right like a ponytail or quiff. It is the key silhouette feature, so keep it on the same side in every pose and mirror whole poses in code if needed.
- **The gasket line** (lid seam) reads as a thin black band, like a collar.

**Face:** on the front of the pot, just below the seam.
- Two **big oval eyes**: white, large black pupils, one round highlight each.
- **Thick black brows** that do most of the acting.
- A small wide mouth that can open into a big "O".
- Pink blush dots appear only when embarrassed or overheated.

**Limbs:** thin black rubber-hose arms and legs.
- **Turmeric-yellow oven-mitt hands.**
- Short legs in **chilli-red canvas sneakers** with white soles.

**Clothing:**
- A **turmeric-yellow cotton scarf with thin chilli-red stripes**, tied at the gasket line, with two ends that flutter.
- The colours are chosen to read as "spices", not as any religious or political colour code. **No saffron-only, green-only or party-colour outfits.**

**Signature props:**
1. **The ladle-mic:** a steel *karchi* (ladle) Seetu interviews families with, like a reporter's mic.
2. **The jhola:** a small jute shoulder bag holding a rolled **route map** and a **recipe diary**.
3. **The stamp:** a wooden stamp for the map; the district counter goes up at each episode's end.

**Colour palette (hex):**

| Element | Hex |
|---|---|
| Steel body (base / highlight / shade) | `#C8CED6` / `#EEF1F4` / `#8F98A3` |
| Ink outline | `#141414` |
| Whistle and sneakers (chilli red) | `#E63B2E` |
| Scarf and mitts (turmeric) | `#F4B400` |
| Eye white / pupil | `#FFFFFF` / `#141414` |
| Blush (overheated) | `#F7A1A1` |
| Steam | `#FFFFFF` at 80% alpha |

**Silhouette test:** filled black, Seetu must still read as "the cooker guy" from the handle, whistle and scarf ends. Every new pose is checked this way before it is approved.

**Drawing style:** flat 2D cut-out cartoon.
- Thick, even black outlines; flat colours with one soft shade side.
- Kid-friendly and clean, no texture.
- The same style is used for food and backgrounds, so nothing looks like photo-real AI.

## 6. Voice

| Attribute | Direction |
|---|---|
| Tone | Warm, bright and playful, with a "smile in the voice". A slightly airy, *steamy* top end. |
| Pitch | Medium-high (not a chipmunk voice). |
| Accent | **Light, natural Indian English.** Never an exaggerated, comic "Indian accent"; that becomes caricature. Clarity for global viewers comes first. |
| Speed | 2.6–2.9 words per second (about 120–130 words in 45 s). Slow down for dish names. |
| Energy | Starts high on the hook, warms down for dinner, then rises again for the cliffhanger. Questions rise at the end. |
| Pronunciation | Every local dish name gets a phonetic respelling in the script (e.g. *puttu* → "putt-oo") and is checked by the local reviewer. Mispronouncing food is the fastest way to lose local viewers. |
| Non-verbal | Whistles, hisses and lid-rattles are SFX layered on top, not performed by the voice actor. |

The AI and recording setup is in `05-sound/sound-guide.md` §4.

## 7. Backstory (released in small lore drops, never all at once)

- **The kitchen years.** For 30 years Seetu sat on one family's kitchen shelf, whistling three times a day: rice, dal, repeat.
- **The phone calls.** The kids grew up and moved to big cities and abroad. Every week the phone rang: *"Amma, how do you make that curry again?"* Amma always said the same thing: "Put it on for three whistles!"
- **The realisation.** Recipes live inside families, and families are scattering. If nobody writes them down, they disappear.
- **The journey.** Seetu tied on a scarf, grabbed a ladle and a notebook, and set off to eat **breakfast, lunch and dinner with a family in every district of India**, writing down what people *really* eat every day.
- **How Seetu travels:** the way India travels, by train, bus, shared jeep, ferry, boat and a lot of walking.
- **The first stop is Ladakh, "where the trains don't go"**, the top of the map.

**Lore drops for milestones:**
- District 100: we meet Amma's voice on the phone.
- District 250: Seetu's original gasket gets replaced, a comic "surgery" episode.
- Each state finale: Seetu mails a recipe card home.

## 8. Recurring settings (made once per culinary region and reused)

| Setting | What it looks like | Reuse rule |
|---|---|---|
| **The arrival** | District establishing shot, with Seetu arriving by local transport (boat, jeep, bus, ferry, train). | 1 new background per district, or reuse a regional one. Transport sprites are reused across the series. |
| **The family kitchen** | Region-specific: clay pots and a coconut scraper (Kerala); iron stove and brass-and-copper shelves (Ladakh); hearth with a smoking rack above (Nagaland); tawa and chakla-belan (north). | Made **once per culinary region** (about 15–20 kitchens for the whole series); props swapped per district. |
| **The counter** | Close-up counter where Seetu hops up to watch the cooking. | Same as the kitchen. |
| **The meal spot** | How the family actually eats: banana leaf on the floor, steel thali, low table, rice bowls. | Per region. |
| **The map board** | End card: India map, dotted route, stamp and counter "District N / 793". | One asset, animated in code. |
| **The recipe diary** | Close-up notebook page with the 3 dishes. It doubles as the Instagram "save this" recipe card. | One asset; text set in code. |

## 9. Running gags fans will wait for

1. **Whistles count the meals; steam rates the spice.**
   - **Whistles = meal counter:** 1 whistle after breakfast, 2 after lunch, 3 after dinner, so every episode literally ends on "Three whistles — done!".
   - **The Steam-o-meter (spice):** after each dish, 0–3 puffs of steam rise from Seetu's lid. The rating comes from the recipe's chilli content and is labelled *editorial*.
   - Very rarely, extreme heat (Naga king chilli, kanthari) overflows the meter and triggers **ROCKET MODE**: the whistle screams non-stop and Seetu blasts off-screen like a rocket.
   - Fans will comment "Rocket when?". Keep it rare (≤ 1 in 10 episodes) so it stays an event.
   - This is a spice guide, not a taste verdict. Seetu never scores a family's cooking.
2. **"Every day… or just for guests?"**
   - Every episode, Seetu squints at the most impressive dish.
   - Often the answer is "For guests we make *this*!", which is the safe, labelled way to mention festival food.
3. **The extra-helping ambush.**
   - A ladle sneaks more food onto Seetu's plate. "No no no… okay. Just one more."
   - It becomes "Just one more… district!" at the cliffhanger.
4. **Lid-pop.** When the unique fact lands, Seetu's lid pops up with a puff of steam: "mind blown".
5. **The stamp.** The map stamp thuds and the counter ticks up. This gives completionist pleasure and makes each episode feel like progress.
6. **Transport roulette.** Seetu arrives by a different vehicle each time (rowing boat in Alappuzha, shared jeep in Leh, hill bus in Kohima). Viewers guess the next one.

## 10. The first 2 seconds (every episode)

**0.00 s:**
- Hard cut, already in motion.
- The **WHEET!** whistle and steam burst.
- Seetu's lid pops and the eyes snap open, staring at the camera.
- The **hook line**, which is always the episode's most surprising true fact, e.g. "This family farms BELOW sea level."
- Big hook text in the top third.

**1.2 s:** "What's cooking, Alappuzha?" (or the hook flows straight into breakfast).

**Never** open with a logo, a "hi guys" or a slow zoom.

## 11. Rules (do / don't)

- **Do:**
  - Show families respectfully and credit the home cook ("Thank you, Leela chechi!" — only with real names and consent, otherwise generic).
  - Say "many families here…" rather than "everyone here…".
- **Don't:**
  - Put Seetu in a costume of a religion, caste or political group.
  - Use religious symbols as gags.
  - Use stereotyped accents.
  - Call food weird, gross or exotic.
  - Show a brand's logo on Seetu. Sponsors get a separate, labelled segment.
- **Consistency:**
  - One hero design, one pose library.
  - The handle is always on Seetu's right.
  - Colours exactly as the hex table.
  - Every new pose is generated *from the hero image as reference* and passes the silhouette test.

## 12. Originality check

- I checked the design against well-known Indian and global mascots and characters and avoided their signature traits:
  - The Amul girl, Air India's Maharaja, Chhota Bheem, Motu Patlu.
  - Kitchen-appliance brand mascots.
  - Cartoon-glove designs (Seetu has oven mitts, not white gloves).
  - "Cooku with Comali" (a Tamil TV show), which is why the name is not "Cooku".
- No brand jingle is used. The sonic logo is an original three-note whistle motif (see the sound guide).
- **Before launch:** run a trademark search for "Seetu" in classes 9, 16, 25, 28 and 41 (India and US), and check that the YouTube handle @WhatIndiaEats (or your chosen title) is free. This is a short legal step, not a creative one.
